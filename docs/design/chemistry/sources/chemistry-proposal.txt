# General-Purpose Digital Chemistry

## Goal

Replace Antropy's named environmental chemicals and dedicated chemical mechanics with a small general-purpose chemical system.

The world should not intrinsically contain concepts such as food A, food B, toxin, signal, carbon, oxygen, or matrix. Instead, it contains chemical species with low-level physical properties. Cells evolve ways to sense, transport, and transform those species.

Higher-level ecological roles should emerge from use:

* a chemical becomes a **food** when an organism can obtain useful energy or material from it;
* a chemical becomes a **waste product** when it is produced by metabolism and discarded;
* a chemical becomes a **toxin** when its physical properties harm organisms exposed to it;
* a chemical becomes a **signal** when organisms benefit from sensing it;
* chemistry becomes **matrix-like** when accumulated material changes local mobility or transport;
* one organism's waste may become another organism's resource.

The system should remain deterministic, locally causal, materially accountable, and evolvable through small mutations.

---

## 1. Chemical space

There are 256 possible chemical species.

Each species is represented by one byte, interpreted as a coordinate in a 16 × 16 chemical space:

```text
species byte = XXXX YYYY

x = high nibble  // 0–15
y = low nibble   // 0–15
```

Chemical identity is therefore a position `(x, y)` rather than an arbitrary lookup key.

Nearby positions generally have related physical properties and similar affinity for the same cellular machinery. Small genetic mutations can therefore produce small chemical changes.

The chemical space is bounded rather than circular. Mutations or transformations crossing an edge reflect back into the valid range.

---

## 2. Chemical properties

Each chemical coordinate has a small set of intrinsic physical properties derived from deterministic smooth functions over the 2D chemical space.

Initial properties:

```text
U(x, y)  chemical potential
D(x, y)  diffusion coefficient
V(x, y)  viscosity contribution
S(x, y)  membrane-stress contribution
```

These functions are part of the world's chemistry definition and remain fixed for the lifetime of a world.

They should be generated from a small set of low-frequency 2D basis functions with persisted coefficients. This produces a continuous but nontrivial chemical landscape without defining 256 species individually.

The complete chemistry can therefore be visualized as four 16 × 16 heatmaps.

### Chemical potential

`U` represents stored chemical energy.

High-potential chemicals can release usable energy when transformed into lower-potential chemicals. Creating a higher-potential chemical requires cellular energy.

### Diffusion

`D` determines how rapidly a species spreads through open space.

### Viscosity

`V` determines how strongly accumulated extracellular chemistry impedes movement and local transport.

At position `p`:

```text
viscosityLoad(p) = Σ concentration(s, p) × V(s)
```

Mobility and field transport use a bounded monotonic function such as:

```text
mobility = 1 / (1 + viscosityLoad)
```

Low concentrations therefore slow movement slightly. Large accumulations can become effectively wall-like without introducing a separate wall chemical or thresholded wall mechanic.

### Membrane stress

`S` represents a chemical's direct adverse interaction with cells.

```text
stressLoad(p) = Σ concentration(s, p) × S(s)
```

Stress produces bounded physiological damage through the existing damage/accounting model.

Organisms can avoid, tolerate, export, or chemically transform harmful compounds. There is no intrinsic toxin category.

---

## 3. Environmental chemical fields

Each world location may contain multiple chemical species.

The representation should be sparse:

```text
position:
    species 17  → 0.42
    species 93  → 1.70
    species 201 → 0.08
```

Only species with nonzero local concentration require storage.

Each species diffuses according to `D(species)`, modified by local viscosity.

Chemical fields contain conserved material except where explicitly exchanged with an external world source or sink.

World resource patches become generic chemical sources. A source specifies:

```text
species
location / region
supply rate or finite inventory
```

The simulation may therefore begin with several externally supplied chemical species without assigning them biological roles.

---

## 4. Shared chemical affinity

Receptors, transporters, and enzymes all use the same chemical-affinity rule.

Each piece of cellular machinery has a target coordinate `(x, y)`.

Its affinity for a chemical decreases smoothly with Euclidean distance in chemical space:

```text
distance = sqrt((x1-x2)^2 + (y1-y2)^2)

affinity = exp(-(distance² / 2σ²))
```

`σ` is a global chemistry constant in the initial design.

Specificity is therefore not independently evolvable in v1. This keeps the search space small while preserving gradual mutation.

A mutation moving a target from `(5, 7)` to `(6, 7)` changes what the machinery interacts with rather than replacing its function with an unrelated one.

---

## 5. Chemical receptors

Cells sense chemistry through general-purpose receptor slots.

A receptor contains:

```text
targetSpecies: (x, y)
```

For the local chemical mixture, receptor activation is the affinity-weighted sum:

```text
activation =
    Σ concentration(s) × affinity(targetSpecies, s)
```

Each receptor can expose the same existing spatial/temporal observations:

```text
local level
temporal change
body-relative gradient X
body-relative gradient Y
```

The controller therefore receives information such as:

> "chemistry near my receptor centered on `(6, 11)` is increasing to my left"

rather than:

> "toxin A is increasing to my left."

Receptor target coordinates are heritable and mutable.

The number of receptor slots and their mapping into the controller remain part of the controller/genome design rather than the chemistry model itself.

---

## 6. Internal chemical inventory

Cells maintain a sparse internal chemical inventory using the same 256 species.

Transported chemicals enter this inventory before metabolism.

Internal chemical quantity is limited by the cell's existing storage capacity.

The cell therefore has three distinct forms of state:

```text
chemical inventory
usable cellular energy
built cellular material / machinery
```

Chemical identity is retained while material remains in the chemical inventory.

---

## 7. Transporters

Transporters move chemicals across the membrane.

Each transporter has:

```text
targetSpecies: (x, y)
direction: import | export
capacity
```

Transfer applies to nearby species according to the shared affinity function.

Conceptually:

```text
flux(s) =
    transporterAmount
    × transportRate
    × affinity(targetSpecies, s)
    × availableConcentration(s)
```

Flux is bounded by available external/internal material, storage capacity, and transport capacity.

Transport consumes usable cellular energy proportional to transferred amount. This prevents selective concentration and chemical sorting from being free.

Transporters provide the general mechanisms previously represented by separate uptake and secretion systems.

---

## 8. Enzymes and unary reactions

The initial chemistry supports one reaction form:

```text
A → B
```

An enzyme contains:

```text
substrateCenter: (x, y)
productOffset: (dx, dy)
catalyticCapacity
```

For any internal species `A` near the enzyme's substrate center:

```text
B = reflect(A + productOffset)
```

The same enzyme therefore acts on a neighborhood of chemically related substrates and moves each by the same vector through chemical space.

Example:

```text
enzyme:
    substrate center = (5, 8)
    product offset   = (+2, -1)
```

may strongly convert:

```text
(5,8) → (7,7)
```

while also converting nearby species such as:

```text
(5,9) → (7,8)
(4,8) → (6,7)
```

at lower affinity.

This creates evolvable reaction families rather than exact hardcoded reaction pairs.

### Reaction rate

The first implementation should use simple first-order kinetics:

```text
reactionAmount(s) =
    enzymeAmount
    × catalyticRate
    × affinity(substrateCenter, s)
    × internalAmount(s)
    × dt
```

The actual amount is clamped by available substrate and available energy for endergonic reactions.

Michaelis-Menten saturation, inhibition, cofactors, and multi-substrate kinetics are intentionally omitted from the initial model.

---

## 9. Reaction energy

For:

```text
A → B
```

the chemical energy difference is:

```text
ΔU = U(A) - U(B)
```

### Exergonic reaction

If:

```text
ΔU > 0
```

the reaction releases energy.

The cell captures:

```text
captured = ΔU × amount × η
```

where `η < 1` is a global conversion efficiency.

The remainder leaves the modeled chemical system as waste heat.

### Endergonic reaction

If:

```text
ΔU < 0
```

the cell must supply:

```text
required = (-ΔU × amount) / η
```

from usable cellular energy.

The reaction cannot proceed beyond the amount the cell can fund.

This allows organisms to synthesize higher-potential compounds while preventing free energetic cycles.

For any closed reaction cycle, inefficiency guarantees a net energy loss.

---

## 10. Matter conservation

Chemical transformations conserve chemical material exactly:

```text
1 unit A → 1 unit B
```

Reaction identity and energy change, but chemical quantity does not.

Transport also conserves material between internal and external inventories.

External world sources and sinks are explicitly accounted boundary flows.

Existing built cellular material remains a separate conserved material stock. Chemical inventory can be assimilated into cellular material using the existing construction economy, consuming the required usable energy.

On death, cellular material returns to the environment through a configured decomposition species in chemical space. This is a world boundary rule analogous to choosing the chemical composition of external resource sources; it does not assign that species a biological role.

---

## 11. Evolutionary continuity

Chemical machinery should mutate locally.

Typical mutations include:

```text
receptor target:
    (5,7) → (6,7)

transporter target:
    (12,3) → (12,4)

enzyme substrate:
    (8,8) → (7,8)

enzyme product offset:
    (+2,-1) → (+1,-1)

machinery investment:
    small quantitative change
```

Large jumps may exist at a much lower mutation probability, but local mutation should dominate.

Because both chemical properties and machinery affinity vary smoothly through the same 2D space, nearby mutations generally produce nearby functional effects.

---

## 12. Intended emergent interpretations

No simulation component assigns these meanings, but the system should permit them to arise:

### Food

A chemical becomes food when a cell imports it and can convert it into lower-potential products while capturing useful energy and/or assimilating its material.

### Waste

A chemical becomes waste when a cell produces and exports it because retaining or further processing it is not advantageous.

### Cross-feeding

One species may consume another species' exported metabolic product.

### Toxin

A chemical becomes toxic when its membrane-stress properties reduce survival or reproduction of exposed organisms.

### Detoxification

A cell may import or encounter a stressful chemical and transform it into a less stressful species.

### Signal

A chemical becomes a signal when producing it and sensing its local concentration changes behavior in a way that improves reproductive success.

The chemical itself needs no signal-specific physical effect.

### Matrix / barrier

Cells may produce slowly diffusing, high-viscosity compounds. Local accumulation can reduce movement and diffusion enough to form persistent spatial structure.

### Energy storage or synthesis

Cells may spend energy moving material uphill in chemical potential, retain the resulting compound, and later recover some of that energy through a downhill reaction.

---

## 13. Initial scope

The first implementation should include:

* 256 species in a 16 × 16 chemical space;
* sparse extracellular chemical mixtures;
* sparse intracellular chemical inventories;
* smooth fixed chemical-property functions;
* chemical potential;
* species-dependent diffusion;
* viscosity effects;
* membrane stress;
* shared fixed-width affinity;
* general receptors;
* import/export transporters;
* unary enzymatic transformations;
* conserved chemical matter;
* explicit reaction energy accounting;
* local mutation of chemical machinery.

This is sufficient to test whether metabolic niches, cross-feeding, chemical interference, environmental modification, and signaling-like behavior can arise from one common substrate.

---

## 14. Deferred extensions

Extensions should be justified by an observed limitation of the unary chemistry.

Likely future candidates include:

```text
A + B → C
```

for true multi-substrate chemistry, cofactors, neutralization, and reactions between products of different organisms.

Other possible later extensions include evolvable receptor/enzyme bandwidth, passive membrane permeability, chemical binding, catalysts that operate extracellularly, and more complex molecular conservation.

These are not prerequisites for the initial chemistry.

---

## Design principle

The chemistry should contain **properties and transformations, not biological meanings**.

The world defines a continuous chemical landscape. Cells evolve where to sense it, where to move material within it, and how to transform one region of it into another.

Ecological concepts such as resource, waste, toxin, signal, metabolism, cross-feeding, and barrier formation should describe what evolved systems *do*, not what the simulation says a chemical *is*.
