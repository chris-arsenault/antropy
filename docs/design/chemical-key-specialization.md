## Intended consequence

Inherited chemical differences should sometimes produce qualitatively different ways of
living in shared surroundings: gaining a usable substrate, losing protection, changing waste
into food, or changing what an exported substance does to a neighbor. Investigate why ordinary
cells instead retain broad private processing and whether that breadth obscures, permits or
prevents consequential differentiation.

The ecological question spans recognition, transport, transformations, protection, costs,
controller actions, neighbor effects and descendant replacement. It cannot be answered by
lowering one breadth statistic, increasing recognition steepness or counting different keys.
Broad metabolism is not forbidden; assigned specialists and a required strategy count are
not the goal. The concern is the apparent convergence toward broadly similar chemical
relationships despite a mechanism intended to make distinct relationships accessible.

## Competing explanations and investigation

- **What the breadth measures.** Separate per-site compiled recognition, funded capacities,
  expressed activities, external intake and net chemical returns. Several selective enzyme
  programs can jointly process many identities; repeated private cycles can dominate gross
  throughput without being distinct external food sources. Determine whether broad processing
  is generalist recognition, a composition of selective routes, or an accounting description
  of cycling before changing its mathematics.
- **Accessible inheritance.** Examine how the shared mutation law and key representation move
  relative preference and total occupancy, and whether narrow useful relationships are accessible
  and retained through births. Positive bias can no longer erase preference by saturating every
  identity at full strength. A changed key in unused machinery
  is not an expressed ecological change. Do not presume mutation is the cause of colony loss.
- **Physical returns and tradeoffs.** Determine what broad uptake, protection and private
  conversion actually gain and cost under the existing capacity/occupancy laws. Ask whether
  narrow relationships have a conditional advantage, whether broad protection removes meaningful
  exposure differences, and whether paid actions or local mixtures suppress available distinctions.
  Do not introduce a breadth tax or per-role reward merely to force the expected picture.
- **Shared surroundings and neighbors.** Follow contemporaneous local readings, retained
  inventory, transport direction, exact products and exports through another cell's uptake,
  harm or funded growth. Different dominant chemical labels or close export/import profiles
  do not demonstrate transfer. Control exposure and private experience when comparing inherited
  alternatives; preserve negative results.
- **Persistence through replacement.** Assess whether contrasting chemical returns are
  inherited, expressed and maintained among co-located descendants through ordinary source
  renewals. Separate transient differences between individuals from continuing ways of living.
  Geographic separation and genotype counts alone cannot establish the intended roles.
